Take one large assignment and turn it into steps with dates attached.

Work backwards from the due date, not forwards from today. Reserve the last 10–15% of the available time for revision and submission mechanics — formatting, citations, upload, the thing that always takes longer than expected. Students overwhelmingly plan to the point of "finished draft" and then have no time left.

Each milestone needs three things: **a date**, **a deliverable you could show someone**, and **an hour estimate**. "Research" is not a milestone. "Six sources read and quoted into the outline — Saturday, 3 hours" is.

Front-load the part that can block everything else. For a paper that is usually the thesis and source-gathering; for code, the piece with the most unknowns; for a group project, whatever depends on other people replying. Anything that requires someone else's response goes first, with slack after it.

Pull the real details from the assignment if you can read it — rubric, length, source requirements, checkpoint deadlines the professor set. Those checkpoints are milestones you do not get to move.

If Canvas is connected, check what else is due in the same window with `canvas_assignments` and route around it. A plan that schedules eight hours of work into the week of two midterms is not a plan.

End with the single next action, small enough to start today. Not "begin research" — "open the library database and find two sources on X, 30 minutes."

If the timeline genuinely does not fit the remaining time, say so up front and give the realistic version instead of a schedule that requires everything to go right.
