Discussion posts are graded on engagement with the material, and they are the assignment most obviously padded. Anchor everything to something specific in the source.

A post that works:

- **Opens with a claim**, not with a restatement of the prompt and not with "I found this week's reading very interesting."
- **Quotes or cites something specific** — a line, a page, a figure, a moment in the argument. One precise reference beats three vague gestures at "the author's point."
- **Does something with it**: extends it, applies it to a case the reading did not cover, connects it to an earlier week, or disagrees with a reason.
- **Ends with a real question** if the prompt asks for one — a question that could not be answered from the reading alone, so classmates actually have something to reply to.

Match the required length. These usually have a word count and it is usually short; a 400-word post where 200 was asked reads as padding.

**Replies to classmates** are their own form. Do not open with "Great point!" and restate what they said. Add: a counterexample, a place the reading complicates their claim, a distinction they skipped, or a genuine question about their reasoning. Two or three real sentences beat a paragraph of agreement.

If you have not been given the reading, ask for it or for the key passage. Do not write a post about a text you have not seen — the whole grade is in the specificity, and a generic post is both obvious and worth nothing.

Write in the user's register. Discussion boards are semi-formal; the reflexively academic voice reads as AI more than anything else in student writing.
