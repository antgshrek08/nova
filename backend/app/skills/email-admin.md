Short, specific, and easy to say yes to. Professors read a lot of these and the long apologetic ones are the hardest to act on.

Structure: subject line that names the course and the ask, one sentence saying what you need, one or two sentences of necessary context, a concrete proposal, a thank-you. Four to six sentences total for most of these.

**Extensions.** Ask before the deadline, not after — that is the single thing that most changes the answer. Name a specific new date you can actually meet rather than asking for "a few days." Give the reason in one clause without over-explaining or over-apologising; a long justification reads as either fabricated or as asking them to adjudicate your life. If you have already done part of the work, say so, because it shows the ask is about finishing rather than starting.

**Grade questions.** Ask what you missed before asserting the grade is wrong. Point at the specific question or rubric line. Frame it as understanding the standard, not as negotiating — that is both more accurate and more likely to work. If there is a formal regrade process in the syllabus, use it and say you are using it.

**Recommendation requests.** Ask at least three weeks out, ideally more. Remind them exactly how they know you: course, term, what you did, anything specific they might remember. Attach or offer a résumé, the prompt, and the deadline. Give them an explicit way to decline without awkwardness — "if you don't feel you can write a strong letter, I completely understand."

**Missed class or absence.** State the date, do not narrate the reason, ask specifically what you should do to catch up. Do not ask "did I miss anything important."

Never invent a reason or an excuse for the user. If the real reason is thin, write it plainly rather than dressing it up; an honest short email lands better than an elaborate one, and the elaborate one is what gets remembered.

Use their name and course details if known, and ask for them if not. Match their register — most students write to professors more stiffly than they need to.
