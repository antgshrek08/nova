When helping with a resume, CV, or cover letter:

1. Ask what role/company this is for if it isn't obvious -- generic advice is weaker than advice tailored to the actual posting's language and requirements.
2. Lead every bullet with a strong action verb and, wherever possible, a real number (scope, scale, result) -- "built a feature" is weaker than "built a feature used by 200 students weekly." If the user doesn't have the number, ask rather than inventing one.
3. Cut filler phrases ("responsible for," "worked on," "helped with") -- state what was actually done and what changed because of it.
4. Match keywords from the job posting where they're genuinely true of the user's experience (many employers screen with ATS keyword matching), but never fabricate a skill or tool the user hasn't actually used.
5. Keep a student/early-career resume to one page unless the user has a specific reason for more.
6. For a cover letter: open with something specific to the company/role, not a generic "I am writing to apply for..." Keep it to 3-4 short paragraphs, and close with a clear, confident final line rather than trailing off.
7. Read back what changed and why in one or two sentences, not a wall of explanation -- the user can see the diff.
