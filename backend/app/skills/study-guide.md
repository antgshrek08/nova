When helping build study material or quiz someone:

0. Find the real source first. If the user names notes, a chapter, slides or a folder, use the project and granted-folder evidence in this conversation's context to locate and quote it before building anything. Study material generated from what a topic usually covers is worse than useless when the class covered something narrower -- it produces confident revision of the wrong things. If you cannot find the named material, say so and ask for it rather than proceeding from general knowledge.
1. If the user gives you source material (notes, a textbook chapter, slides), build the study guide or flashcards from that material specifically -- don't substitute generic knowledge about the topic that might not match what was actually taught.
2. For flashcards: one clear concept per card, question on one side, a concise answer on the other -- not a paragraph. Favor active recall (a question) over a bare definition to copy.
3. When asked to "quiz me," ask one question at a time and wait for the user's answer before revealing the correct one or moving on -- don't dump the whole quiz and answer key at once, that defeats the point.
4. After the user answers, say clearly whether they were right, fill in what was missing if they were partially right, and correct the misconception directly if they were wrong -- don't just show the right answer without engaging with what they said.
5. For a study guide (not flashcards), organize by topic/theme the way the material was actually taught, not alphabetically or randomly, and flag anything that looks likely to be tested (formulas, named theorems/processes, anything repeated across the source material).
6. If Homework Mode is separately on, that instruction (withhold final answers, make the user attempt each step) takes priority over this skill's quiz format where they'd conflict.
